If you last went to Thailand in 2025, the rules have changed. In **September 2026**, Thailand cut the visa-free stay for Malaysians from **60 days to 30 days**.

For most of us doing a long weekend in Hatyai or a week in Bangkok, nothing really changes. But if you planned a long stay, a "workcation" or a business trip, read this first.

## What changed in September 2026

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Visa-free stay | 60 days | **30 days** |
| Allowed purpose | Tourism, plus short business and work activities | **Tourism only** |
| Extension | Possible | One extension of up to 30 days, at immigration's discretion |
| Land border entries | — | Max 2 per year for most countries, **but Malaysians are exempt** |

The new rules were published in Thailand's Royal Gazette on 31 August 2026 and apply to Malaysia and about 60 other countries and territories. If you entered Thailand before the change, you keep the stay you were given on arrival. ([Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2605517), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/01/thailand-cuts-visa-free-stays-from-60-days-to-30-days-for-60-countries-including-malaysia))

<Callout type="tip" title="Good news for Hatyai and Danok trips">
Some countries now have a limit of 2 land-border visa-free entries a year. Malaysians are exempt, so weekend drives to Hatyai via Bukit Kayu Hitam are unaffected.
</Callout>

## What you need to enter Thailand

1. **Passport** valid for at least 6 months from your arrival date.
2. **TDAC (Thailand Digital Arrival Card)**, submitted online within 3 days before you arrive. It's free. See our [step-by-step TDAC guide](/blog/tdac-thailand-digital-arrival-card-malaysians/).
3. **Return or onward ticket** within 30 days. Officers can ask for it, and airlines sometimes check at check-in.
4. **Accommodation details**: your hotel address, needed for the TDAC.
5. **Enough money for your stay.** Immigration can ask, so have a bank card and some cash.

## Staying longer than 30 days

You have three options:

- **Extend once** at a Thai immigration office for up to 30 more days. Bring your passport, a photo, the application form and the fee. Go a few days before your 30 days end, not on the last day.
- **Apply for a visa before you go** (for example a tourist visa) through the Royal Thai Embassy in Kuala Lumpur or the e-Visa system.
- **Leave and re-enter.** Malaysians aren't limited on land entries, but repeated back-to-back "border runs" can draw questions from immigration officers.

<Callout type="warn" title="Don't overstay">
Overstaying in Thailand means a daily fine, and longer overstays can lead to a ban on re-entering. Count your days from your entry stamp.
</Callout>

## Business trips

The new visa exemption is **tourism only**. Meetings, conferences and site visits now need a business (Non-Immigrant B) visa. If your company is sending you to Bangkok, check with the Royal Thai Embassy before you fly.
