**"Jom, cuti-cuti Malaysia!"** *Cuti* means holiday, and **Cuti-cuti Malaysia** is the Tourism Malaysia slogan every Malaysian grew up with. It's how we say: take your holiday at home.

KL is only the start. In under two hours, by plane or road, you can be in a UNESCO-listed heritage town, on a duty-free island, in cool tea country or in the Borneo rainforest. Here are nine places to go next, with how to get there from KL and when to go.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check the monsoon first">
Malaysia has two coasts and two rainy seasons. From about <strong>November to March</strong>, the northeast monsoon hits the <strong>east coast</strong>, and many resorts on Perhentian, Redang and Tioman close. That's when the <strong>west coast</strong> (Penang, Langkawi) has its best weather. From about April to October it's the other way round.
</Callout>

## 1. Penang: street art and the food capital

**George Town** is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (with Melaka, since 2008): a grid of colourful shophouses, clan houses, mosques, temples and churches built by traders from China, India, the Malay world and Europe.

- **Do:** hunt the street art. The famous murals by Lithuanian artist Ernest Zacharevic, including *Children on a Bicycle*, were painted for the 2012 George Town Festival. Then visit **Khoo Kongsi** clan house, ride the funicular up **Penang Hill**, and see **Kek Lok Si** temple.
- **Eat:** char kuey teow, assam laksa, nasi kandar, cendol. See our [Malaysian food guide](/blog/malaysian-food-guide/).
- **From KL:** about 1 hour by plane, or the ETS train to Butterworth and the ferry to George Town. About 4 hours by road.
- **Best time:** December to February is the driest.

## 2. Melaka: history you can walk through

Malaysia's historic port city. The Portuguese arrived in 1511, then the Dutch and the British, and you can see all of them in a single walk: the red Dutch **Stadthuys**, the Portuguese gate of **A Famosa**, and Peranakan townhouses on **Jonker Street**.

- **Do:** the **Jonker Walk night market** (weekend evenings), a **Melaka River cruise** after dark, and a Peranakan heritage house.
- **Eat:** chicken rice balls, Nyonya laksa, cendol with gula Melaka.
- **From KL:** about 2 hours by bus or car. Easy as a day trip, better with one night.
- **Best time:** all year. Go on a weekend for the night market, a weekday to avoid crowds.

## 3. Langkawi: the duty-free island

An archipelago of 99 islands off Kedah, with **duty-free shopping** (since 1987), beaches, mangroves and rainforest peaks.

- **Do:** ride the **SkyCab** cable car up Gunung Mat Cincang to the curved **Sky Bridge**, take a mangrove boat tour, island-hop by speedboat, and watch the sunset at **Pantai Cenang**.
- **From KL:** about 1 hour by plane, or a ferry from Kuala Perlis or Kuala Kedah.
- **Best time:** roughly December to April is driest. The SkyCab and Sky Bridge can close in bad weather, so go early on a clear day.

## 4. Ipoh: cave temples and white coffee

A laid-back old tin-mining town with colonial buildings, murals in the old town, and **Buddhist cave temples** built into the limestone hills.

- **Eat:** Ipoh white coffee, bean sprout chicken, and dim sum for breakfast.
- **From KL:** about 2 to 2.5 hours on the ETS train. Book ahead on weekends.

## 5. Cameron Highlands: tea country

Tea plantations, strawberry farms and the misty **Mossy Forest**, at around 1,500 m. Cool enough for a jacket at night, which is a novelty in Malaysia.

- **From KL:** about 3.5 to 4 hours by road. A guided day trip saves you the winding drive.
- **Tip:** avoid long weekends and school holidays, when the roads up get jammed.

## 6. Genting Highlands: cool air and theme parks

A hilltop resort about an hour from KL, with the **Awana SkyWay** cable car, the **Genting SkyWorlds** theme park, a casino and premium outlets. Popular with families and anyone who wants a break from the heat.

## 7. Sabah: Mount Kinabalu and orangutans

Malaysian Borneo's northern state, and one of its biggest adventures.

- **Climb Mount Kinabalu** (4,095 m): you need a **permit and a licensed guide**, and most climbers stay overnight at Laban Rata. Book months ahead.
- **See orangutans** at the **Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre** near Sandakan, and pygmy elephants and proboscis monkeys on the **Kinabatangan River**.
- **Island-hop** from Kota Kinabalu to the islands of Tunku Abdul Rahman Park, 15–20 minutes by boat.
- **From KL:** about 2.5 hours by plane to Kota Kinabalu. Foreign visitors go through immigration again on arrival.

## 8. Sarawak: rainforest, caves and Kuching

- **Kuching** is a relaxed riverside city famous for its cat statues, and for Sarawak laksa.
- **Semenggoh Nature Reserve** is home to semi-wild orangutans. Come at feeding time for the best chance of seeing them.
- **Bako National Park** has proboscis monkeys and coastal trails, and **Gunung Mulu National Park** (UNESCO) has some of the world's largest caves.
- **From KL:** about 1 hour 45 minutes by plane to Kuching.

## 9. The east coast islands: Perhentian, Redang, Tioman

Clear water, coral reefs and turtles: some of the best snorkelling and diving in Malaysia.

- **Best time:** roughly **March to October**.
- **November to February:** many resorts, ferries and dive shops **close** for the northeast monsoon. Don't plan a December island trip here.

## When to go: a quick guide

| Destination | Best months | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Penang, Langkawi | Dec–Apr | Heavier rain around Sep–Oct |
| Melaka, Ipoh, KL | All year | Expect afternoon storms |
| Cameron, Genting | All year | Long weekends (traffic) |
| Perhentian, Redang, Tioman | Mar–Oct | Nov–Feb (many resorts close) |
| Sabah, Sarawak | All year, drier ~Mar–Sep | Check Kinabalu permits early |

## Getting around Malaysia

- **Fly** for Penang, Langkawi, Sabah and Sarawak. Domestic flights from KLIA, KLIA2 or Subang are short and often cheap.
- **ETS trains** from KL Sentral run north to Ipoh and Butterworth (for Penang).
- **Buses** from TBS (Terminal Bersepadu Selatan) cover Melaka and most towns.
- **Private transfers** are easiest for groups, families and anyone with luggage or sports gear.
