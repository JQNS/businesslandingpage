KL to Singapore is one of the most travelled routes in Asia, and there's no single best way to do it. It depends on whether you care more about time, money or comfort.

Here's how the four options compare, and the two pieces of paperwork that catch people out.

## The 4 options at a glance

| | Door to door | Rough cost (one way) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flight** | ~4 hrs | Varies a lot, cheapest when booked early | Short trips, people who hate jams |
| **Bus** | 5–7+ hrs | Usually the cheapest | Budget travellers, students |
| **ETS + Shuttle Tebrau** | 5–6 hrs | ETS from about RM70–110 + RM5 shuttle | Comfort, working on the way, no jam risk |
| **Drive** | 4–6+ hrs | Petrol, tolls, VEP, Singapore parking | Families with lots of bags |

Prices change with the day and season. Expect higher fares on Fridays, public holidays and during school holidays.

## 1. Flight: fastest in the air

The flight from KLIA to Changi is about an hour, and there are lots of departures a day. Once you add the trip to KLIA, check-in and the ride into Singapore, it's closer to 4 hours door to door.

**Pick it if:** you're only going for a weekend, or you found a cheap fare. Book early for December and Chinese New Year.

## 2. Bus: cheapest and most flexible

Express buses leave KL many times a day, from TBS and several pick-up points around the city. You get off the bus at both immigration checkpoints, so keep your passport and bag with you.

**Pick it if:** you want the lowest price. **Watch out for:** Causeway and Second Link jams on Friday evenings, Sunday nights and the start of school holidays. A 5-hour trip can turn into 8.

## 3. Train: ETS to JB, then Shuttle Tebrau

Since December 2025, KTM's **ETS** runs all the way from **KL Sentral to JB Sentral** in about 4 to 5 hours. From JB Sentral, the **Shuttle Tebrau** crosses to Woodlands in about 5 minutes for **RM5** one way for Malaysians.

**Pick it if:** you want a comfortable seat, no traffic and no airport. **Watch out for:** Shuttle Tebrau tickets sell out at peak times. Book both legs on KTMB's KITS site or app as soon as you know your dates, and leave a buffer between the two trains.

<Callout type="info" title="RTS Link: coming in 2027">
The JB–Woodlands **RTS Link** will add a second 5-minute train across the strait. It's now expected to open in **February 2027**. Fares haven't been announced. See [RTS Link: opening date and fare](/blog/rts-link-jb-singapore/).
</Callout>

## 4. Drive: best for families

Driving gives you the most freedom, and the 300+ km of highway is straightforward. The hard parts are the border and the paperwork.

- Your car needs a valid **Vehicle Entry Permit (VEP)** and an Autopass card.
- From **6 October 2026**, you can't submit the SG Arrival Card for a foreign-registered car without a valid VEP.
- Parking in central Singapore is expensive. Many families park at the hotel and use the MRT.

## The SG Arrival Card: free, do it before you go

Everyone entering Singapore, including Malaysians, must submit the **SG Arrival Card**. It's **free** on the official ICA website or the **MyICA app**. Submit it within the 3 days before you arrive.

<Callout type="warn" title="Don't pay for it">
Search results are full of lookalike websites that charge a "processing fee". The official card is free. Use only the ICA site or the MyICA app.
</Callout>
