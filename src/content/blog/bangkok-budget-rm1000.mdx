## The RM1,000 budget

| | Tight | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|
| ✈️ Return flight (budget airline, off-peak) | RM300 | RM450 |
| 🏨 2 nights, 3-star hotel near BTS (your half of a shared room) | RM150 | RM250 |
| 🚆 Airport transfers (train) + BTS/MRT | RM50 | RM80 |
| 🍜 Food (street food + one nice meal) | RM120 | RM180 |
| 🛕 Sights (Grand Palace, Wat Arun, one rooftop) | RM100 | RM150 |
| 📶 Thailand eSIM | RM25 | RM30 |
| **Total before shopping** | **RM745** | **RM1,140** |
| 🛍️ Left for shopping | ~RM255 | — |

So **RM1,000 works on the tight plan**, with room for some shopping. On the comfortable plan, or travelling solo with a room to yourself, budget around **RM1,200–1,400**.

## 1. Flights: where you save (or lose) the most

- **Book 6–8 weeks ahead** and avoid Malaysian school holidays, long weekends, Songkran and New Year.
- **Fly mid-week.** Tuesday and Wednesday are often cheapest.
- **Cabin bag only on the way out**, then add one checked bag on the way home for your shopping. It's cheaper to add it online than at the counter.
- Most budget flights land at **Don Mueang (DMK)**. Read [how to get from the airport to the city](/blog/bangkok-airport-to-city/) before you land.

More detail in [KL to Bangkok: cheapest ways to get there](/blog/kl-to-bangkok/).

## 2. Hotel: stay near the BTS

Your hotel location matters more than the hotel itself. **Stay within walking distance of a BTS or MRT station**, and you'll save on Grab every day and won't get stuck in Bangkok traffic.

- **Sukhumvit (Asok / Nana / Phrom Phong):** food, nightlife, easy trains. Best all-rounder.
- **Pratunam / Ratchathewi:** cheapest for shoppers, close to Platinum Mall and Pratunam Market.
- **Silom / Saladaeng:** good value, close to the river and Lumpini Park.

## 3. Getting around for less

- **Airport to city by train:** the Airport Rail Link from Suvarnabhumi, or the SRT Red Line from Don Mueang, costs a fraction of a Grab.
- **BTS and MRT** cover most places tourists go. Pay with a contactless card where accepted, or buy single tickets.
- **Grab** is great late at night or with heavy bags. Split the fare.
- Avoid unmetered taxis and tuk-tuks that offer "special" tours. They're how most first-timers overpay.

## 4. Food: eat like a local

Street food and food courts are the cheapest and often the best food in Bangkok. A plate of noodles or rice is usually **฿50–100 (RM6–13)**. Mall food courts like the one at Terminal 21 are cheap, clean and air-conditioned.

Muslim travellers: see our [halal food guide to Bangkok](/blog/halal-food-bangkok/). There are plenty of good, cheap halal options.

## 5. Sights: pay for the big ones, skip the rest

- **Grand Palace:** the big ticket at **฿500 (≈RM62)**, and worth it once.
- **Wat Arun and Wat Pho:** cheaper, and you can walk or take the river boat between them.
- **Free:** Chatuchak Weekend Market, Lumpini Park, Chinatown at night, Jodd Fairs night market.

Book attraction tickets and rooftop bars online ahead of time. They're often cheaper than at the gate, and you skip the queue.

## Where Malaysians overspend

1. **Peak-date flights.** The same flight can cost double during school holidays.
2. **Grab everywhere.** Taking the train for long trips saves a lot over 3 days.
3. **Bad exchange rates.** Changing money at the airport counter costs the most. Change a small amount there, then use a city money changer or a card with low fees.
4. **Airport baggage fees.** Buy checked baggage online, and weigh your bag before you leave the hotel.
