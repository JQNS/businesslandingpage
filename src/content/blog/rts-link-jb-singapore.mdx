Anyone who has sat in a Causeway jam knows why the **RTS Link** matters. It's a 4 km train from Johor Bahru to Woodlands that takes about **5 minutes**, with immigration done at the station.

If you were planning to ride it this December, though, you'll have to wait a bit longer. Here's the latest opening date, what we know about the fare, and how to cross until it opens.

<Callout type="warn" title="Not opening in December 2026">
Many older articles still say "end of 2026". In October 2026, the operator RTS Operations said passenger service is now expected in **February 2027**, to leave time for safety certification and approvals from both governments. Singapore's Transport Minister said the aim is to open before Hari Raya Puasa (March 2027). We'll update this page when the exact date is announced.
</Callout>

## What we know so far

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **Route** | Bukit Chagar (JB) to Woodlands North (Singapore), about 4 km |
| **Ride time** | About 5 minutes |
| **Capacity** | Up to 10,000 passengers an hour in each direction |
| **Immigration** | Both countries' checks at your departure station, so you clear once |
| **Singapore connection** | Woodlands North is on the Thomson–East Coast MRT line |
| **JB connection** | Bukit Chagar is close to JB Sentral and the city centre |
| **Fare** | Not announced. About 5 to 7 per trip has been mentioned as an estimate |
| **Opening date** | Expected February 2027, to be announced jointly by both transport ministers |

## Why it's a big deal

Today, getting from JB to Singapore during peak hours can mean an hour or more on a bus or in your car, plus two separate immigration stops. With the RTS Link, you clear Malaysian and Singapore immigration once, at the station you leave from, then ride 5 minutes and walk out.

For Malaysians it makes Singapore easier for day trips, concerts and shopping. It should also take some pressure off the Causeway.

## How to get from JB to Singapore now

Until the RTS opens, these are your options:

| Option | Time (no jam) | Cost | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KTM Shuttle Tebrau** (JB Sentral → Woodlands) | ~5 min ride | RM5 one way for Malaysians | Fastest way across when you have a ticket |
| **Cross-border bus** (e.g. from JB Sentral / Larkin) | 30 min to 2+ hrs | A few ringgit | Cheap and flexible, but stuck in jams |
| **Drive** | 20 min to 2+ hrs | Tolls, VEP, petrol | Families with lots of bags |

Shuttle Tebrau tickets sell out at peak times, especially on Friday evenings and at the start of school holidays. Book on KTMB's official app or site (KITS) as soon as your dates are fixed.

<Callout type="info" title="Driving in? You need a VEP first">
From 6 October 2026, drivers of foreign-registered cars must have a valid **Vehicle Entry Permit (VEP)** before the SG Arrival Card system will accept their submission. Sort out your VEP before the trip.
</Callout>

## Don't forget the SG Arrival Card

Every Malaysian visitor to Singapore needs to submit the **SG Arrival Card**. It's **free** on the official ICA website or the MyICA app, and you can submit it within the 3 days before you arrive. Sites that charge a fee are third parties, so skip them.
