Bangkok is one of the best food cities in Asia, and Muslim travellers don't have to miss out. Thailand has a long Thai-Muslim food tradition: massaman curry, khao mok gai (Thai chicken biryani), roti and beef noodle soup.

The trick is knowing **where** to go. Here are the areas Malaysians head for, and how to check what's halal.

## How to check if food is halal in Thailand

Look for the **CICOT logo**: a green and white certification placard from the **Central Islamic Council of Thailand**, the country's halal certification body.

Small family-run Thai-Muslim restaurants may not display it but are Muslim-owned. If you're unsure, ask: "Halal mai?" (Is it halal?). Staff in these areas are used to the question.

<Callout type="warn" title="Street food isn't halal by default">
Many Thai dishes use pork, lard or pork-based stock, and stalls often share woks and utensils. Stick to Muslim-run stalls or the areas below.
</Callout>

## 1. Charoen Krung and Haroon Mosque: Thai-Muslim classics

The old riverside area around **Haroon Mosque** (Charoen Krung Soi 36 and Soi 42) is Bangkok's best-known Thai-Muslim food neighbourhood, with family kitchens, khao mok gai stalls and halal boat noodles.

- **Home Cuisine Islamic** (Charoen Krung Soi 36): a family restaurant serving Thai-Muslim curries, biryani and roti since 2000, with a Michelin Bib Gourmand listing. Try the massaman curry and the roti. **Cash only.**

<Callout type="info" title="The famous Muslim Restaurant has closed">
The historic **Muslim Restaurant** on Charoen Krung closed in September 2024 after about 90 years. Older blogs still list it, so don't make the trip for it.
</Callout>

**Getting there:** the Chao Phraya Express Boat to Oriental or Si Phraya pier, or the BTS to Saphan Taksin and a short walk.

## 2. Soi Arab (Sukhumvit Soi 3/1): Middle Eastern food late at night

Near Nana BTS station, **Soi Arab** is a lane of Arab, Turkish, Egyptian and Indian-Pakistani restaurants, many open late. Good for grilled meats, shawarma and Arabic coffee after a day of shopping.

**Getting there:** BTS to Nana, then walk.

## 3. Silom: weekday lunch near the office towers

Around Silom, Thai-Muslim curry-and-rice shops serve office workers at lunch: quick, cheap and filling. Some open on weekdays only, so go at lunchtime.

## Must-try Thai-Muslim dishes

| Dish | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Massaman curry** | Mild, rich curry with potatoes, peanuts and beef or chicken |
| **Khao mok gai** | Thai-style chicken biryani, often with a sweet-sour green sauce |
| **Roti** | Flatbread, savoury with curry or sweet with banana and condensed milk |
| **Beef noodle soup** | Halal take on Bangkok's classic boat noodles |
| **Satay** | Thai satay with peanut sauce and a cucumber relish |

## Tips for Muslim travellers in Bangkok

- **Prayer:** Bangkok has many mosques. Large malls often have prayer rooms, so ask at the information counter.
- **Hotels:** some hotels offer halal breakfasts or Muslim-friendly facilities. A Bangkok hotel was promoted as [Thailand's first halal hotel](https://bangkokpost.com/travel/1074309/first-top-class-halal-hotel-draws-muslim-tourists). Our full Muslim-friendly hotel guide is coming soon.
- **Plan your days by area:** pair Charoen Krung with Wat Arun and the river, and Soi Arab with Sukhumvit shopping. See our [3-day Bangkok itinerary](/blog/bangkok-3-day-itinerary/).
