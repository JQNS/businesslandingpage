## 1. Ayutthaya: Thailand's old capital

Ayutthaya was the capital of Siam for over 400 years. Its ruined temples, with Buddha heads, brick towers and huge reclining Buddhas, are a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. Most tours also stop at the **Bang Pa-In Summer Palace** and a floating market.

- **Go on a tour if** you want everything in one day without working out tuk-tuks between temples.
- **Go on your own if** you're happy to take the train from Bangkok and rent a bicycle or tuk-tuk in Ayutthaya.

## 2. Damnoen Saduak floating market + Maeklong railway market

This is the classic Bangkok photo day. Wooden boats loaded with fruit and noodles on the canals at **Damnoen Saduak**, then **Maeklong**, where market stalls fold back their awnings a few times a day so a train can roll through the middle.

The two markets are far apart and public transport between them is slow, so **a tour is the easy way**. Leave early: the floating market is best in the morning.

## 3. Erawan Falls + the Bridge on the River Kwai (Kanchanaburi)

**Erawan Falls** is a seven-tier waterfall with turquoise pools you can swim in. Most tours pair it with the **Bridge on the River Kwai** and the history of the WWII "Death Railway", one of the most moving stops in Thailand.

It's a **long day (about 12 hours)**, and reviewers say it can feel rushed. Most people only reach tier 4 or 5. If you want to swim and hike to the top, think about staying a night in Kanchanaburi.

## 4. Pattaya: the easy beach day

About two hours away by bus or car, Pattaya works as a day trip: the beach, the Nong Nooch garden, the Tiffany's cabaret show, or a boat to Koh Larn island. ## 5. Hua Hin: the royal beach town

Hua Hin is calmer and more grown-up than Pattaya, with long beaches, night markets and seafood. At about three hours each way it's a long day, so most people **stay a night**. A private car is the most comfortable way to get there.
