## When to stop training outdoors

Malaysia's Health Ministry advises the public to **reduce strenuous outdoor activity when the API goes above 100**, and says outdoor sports should be postponed or moved indoors at that level. The reason is simple: hard exercise makes you breathe faster and deeper, so you pull far more haze particles (PM2.5) into your lungs than when you're resting.

| API | What it means | What to do with your workout |
|---|---|---|
| **0–50** | Good | Train outdoors as normal |
| **51–100** | Moderate | Outdoors is OK for most people. Sensitive groups take it easy |
| **101–200** | Unhealthy | Move strenuous exercise indoors |
| **201–300** | Very unhealthy | Indoors only, and keep it moderate |
| **300+** | Hazardous | Rest, or very light indoor exercise only |

Check the reading for **your area** before you head out, not just the KL average. API can vary a lot from one district to the next. For what the numbers mean for kids and schools, see our [haze guide for Malaysia and Singapore](/blog/haze-malaysia-singapore-guide/).

<Callout type="warn" title="Don't run in an N95">
A mask is for walking to your car, not for intervals. N95 masks make hard breathing uncomfortable and lose their seal when you sweat. Move the run to a treadmill instead.
</Callout>

## 5 ways to keep training indoors

### 1. Swap your outdoor run for a treadmill or rower

If you're training for a race, keep your running volume on a treadmill. Set the incline to 1% to make it feel closer to running outside. A rower or SkiErg works your cardio without pounding your legs.

### 2. Spin and indoor cycling

Weekend cyclists lose their group rides first when the haze hits. A 45-minute spin class is the closest indoor replacement for a hard ride, and KL has plenty of cycling studios in Bangsar, Mont Kiara and the city centre.

### 3. HYROX-style and strength classes

Haze season is a good time to build strength. HYROX-style classes mix running on treadmills with sleds, rowers and wall balls, all indoors. If you're racing [HYROX Kuala Lumpur](/blog/hyrox-kuala-lumpur-2026-guide/) in December, see [where to train for HYROX in KL](/blog/hyrox-training-kuala-lumpur/).

### 4. Reformer Pilates and yoga

On the worst haze days, a low-impact session keeps you moving without heavy breathing. Reformer Pilates has taken off in KL. Our [beginner's guide to reformer Pilates in KL](/blog/reformer-pilates-kl-beginners/) explains what to expect in your first class.

### 5. Home workouts

When the API goes very high, stay home. Close the windows, run your air purifier and do 20–30 minutes of bodyweight work: squats, lunges, push-ups, planks and mobility.

## Haze training tips

1. **Check the API before every session**, not once a day. It changes hourly.
2. **Choose studios inside malls or office towers.** Their central air-conditioning and closed doors usually mean cleaner air.
3. **Drink more water.** Haze dries out your throat, and so does a hard class.
4. **Ease off if you feel it.** Coughing, a tight chest or unusual breathlessness are signs to stop.
5. **Keep your recovery runs indoors too.** "Easy" outdoor jogs still mean breathing haze for 30+ minutes.

<Callout type="tip" title="Asthma or a heart condition?">
Talk to your doctor before training hard during haze season, and keep your inhaler with you in class. This guide is general information, not medical advice.
</Callout>
