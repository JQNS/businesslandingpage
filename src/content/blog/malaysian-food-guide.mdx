Ask any Malaysian what they miss most when they're abroad and the answer is always the same: **the food**. Malay, Chinese, Indian, Peranakan and Bornean kitchens have been borrowing from each other for centuries, and the result is one of the best food countries on Earth, mostly eaten at plastic tables for a few ringgit.

Here are 25 dishes to try, grouped so you can tick them off over a weekend in KL or a longer trip around the country.

## How Malaysians eat

- **Hawker centres and kopitiams** (old-school coffee shops) are where the best food is. Find a seat, note your **table number**, and order from different stalls. They bring the food to you. Drinks are often ordered from a separate drinks auntie or uncle.
- **Mamak restaurants** are Indian-Muslim, casual, often open until late or 24 hours, and the heart of Malaysian social life.
- **"Makan"** means eat. "Dah makan?" (eaten yet?) is how many Malaysians say hello.
- **Eat with your right hand** at banana leaf and nasi kandar places if you like. Locals do. Spoons and forks are always available.
- **"Tapau"** means takeaway. Drinks to go often come in a plastic bag with a straw.

<Callout type="info" title="Eating halal in Malaysia">
Malay and mamak restaurants are halal. Many Chinese restaurants and stalls serve pork. Look for the <strong>JAKIM halal logo</strong>, Malaysia's official certification. A "pork-free" or "no pork no lard" sign means no pork is served, but the place isn't halal certified.
</Callout>

## Rice dishes

### 1. Nasi lemak
The unofficial national dish: rice steamed in **coconut milk and pandan**, with **sambal**, crispy **ikan bilis** (anchovies), peanuts, cucumber and egg. Add **ayam goreng** (fried chicken) or **rendang**. Malaysians eat it for breakfast, lunch and supper. The simplest version comes wrapped in banana leaf and paper (*nasi lemak bungkus*) for a few ringgit.

### 2. Nasi kandar
A Penang Tamil-Muslim classic: rice with your pick of curries, fried chicken, squid and vegetables, then the cook floods the plate with a mix of curry gravies (ask for *kuah campur*). Queues at the famous Penang shops can go round the block.

### 3. Banana leaf rice
South Indian rice served on a banana leaf with vegetables, papadam, pickles and your choice of curry: fish head, mutton, chicken varieties. When you're done, **fold the leaf towards you** to show you enjoyed the meal. Best in **Brickfields**, KL's Little India.

### 4. Hainanese chicken rice
Poached or roasted chicken over rice cooked in chicken stock, with chilli-garlic sauce and ginger. In **Melaka** it's served as **chicken rice balls**.

### 5. Nasi kerabu
The blue rice of **Kelantan**, coloured with butterfly pea flowers and served with fresh herbs, fish crackers, salted egg and grilled chicken or fish.

## Noodles

### 6. Char kuey teow
Flat rice noodles wok-fried over very high heat with prawns, cockles, egg, bean sprouts and chives. The "wok hei" (breath of the wok) smokiness is everything. **Penang's** version is the most famous. Halal versions are easy to find.

### 7. Curry laksa
Noodles in a rich coconut curry broth with tofu puffs, prawns or chicken, and sambal. KL's favourite.

### 8. Assam laksa (Penang laksa)
Completely different: a **sour, spicy fish broth** with tamarind, mackerel, pineapple, mint and a sticky prawn paste (*hae ko*). It's love or hate. We love it.

### 9. Sarawak laksa
From Kuching: rice vermicelli in a broth of spiced Sarawak laksa paste and coconut milk, with prawns, omelette strips, chicken and lime. Many Sarawakians eat it for breakfast.

### 10. Pan mee
Hand-torn or thin noodles with minced pork, anchovies and greens, in soup or dry with chilli (*chilli pan mee*, a KL favourite).

### 11. Wantan mee
Thin egg noodles with char siu (barbecued pork) and dumplings, usually served dry with soy and a bowl of soup on the side.

### 12. Mee goreng mamak
Yellow noodles fried at the mamak with potato, tofu, egg, chilli and lime. The late-night classic, along with **Maggi goreng** (fried instant noodles).

### 13. Kolo mee
Kuching's springy noodles tossed in lard (or oil), with char siu and minced meat. Simple and addictive.

## Breads, grills and snacks

### 14. Roti canai
Flaky, stretched flatbread griddled until crisp, with dhal and curry for dipping. Try **roti telur** (with egg), **roti bawang** (onion) or **roti tisu**, a tall paper-thin cone dusted with sugar.

### 15. Satay
Skewers of marinated chicken or beef grilled over charcoal, with peanut sauce, cucumber, onion and **ketupat** (pressed rice cakes). **Kajang**, near KL, is Malaysia's satay town.

### 16. Murtabak
Pan-fried bread stuffed with minced meat, egg and onion, served with curry and pickled onions. A Ramadan bazaar favourite.

### 17. Apam balik
A thick, crispy pancake folded over crushed peanuts, sugar and sweetcorn. Night market dessert of choice.

### 18. Kuih
Bite-sized Malay and Nyonya cakes in every colour: **kuih lapis** (layered), **onde-onde** (pandan balls that burst with palm sugar), **seri muka**. Buy a mix in the morning.

## Meat and curries

### 19. Rendang
Beef or chicken slow-cooked in coconut milk and spices until almost dry and deeply flavoured. A Hari Raya essential.

### 20. Bak kut teh
"Meat bone tea": pork ribs simmered in a herbal broth, eaten with rice and youtiao (fried dough). **Klang** is its home. Not halal, but chicken versions exist.

### 21. Ikan bakar
Fish grilled in banana leaf with sambal. Best by the sea or at a night market.

## Desserts and drinks

### 22. Cendol
Shaved ice with **gula Melaka** (palm sugar syrup), coconut milk and green pandan jelly. The best thing to eat on a hot afternoon.

### 23. Ais kacang (ABC)
Shaved ice piled with red beans, sweetcorn, jelly and syrup in bright colours. ABC stands for *air batu campur*, mixed ice.

### 24. Durian
The "king of fruits". Creamy, custardy and very strong-smelling. **Musang King** is the most prized variety. The main season is around June to August. Eat it at the roadside stalls: it's banned on public transport and in most hotels.

### 25. Teh tarik and kopi
**Teh tarik** is sweet milk tea "pulled" between two cups for a frothy top. **Ipoh white coffee** is roasted with margarine for a smooth, nutty cup. Learn the coffee-shop code: **kopi** (with condensed milk), **kopi O** (black with sugar), **kopi C** (with evaporated milk), add **ais** for iced, **kosong** for no sugar. And try a **Milo dinosaur**: iced Milo with a mountain of Milo powder on top.

## Where to eat in Kuala Lumpur

| Area | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Jalan Alor**, Bukit Bintang | Evening hawker street: satay, grilled seafood, noodles |
| **Petaling Street**, Chinatown | Chinese hawker food, old kopitiams |
| **Kampung Baru** | Malay food: nasi lemak, nasi kerabu and Ramadan bazaars, with the KL skyline behind it |
| **Brickfields** (Little India) | Banana leaf rice, Indian sweets, thosai |
| **Any mamak, anytime** | Roti canai and teh tarik at 1am |

## Eat your way around Malaysia

- **Penang:** char kuey teow, assam laksa, nasi kandar, cendol. Many Malaysians call it the food capital.
- **Ipoh:** white coffee, bean sprout chicken (*tauge ayam*), and dim sum for breakfast.
- **Melaka:** chicken rice balls, Nyonya laksa, and cendol with gula Melaka.
- **Kelantan and Terengganu:** nasi kerabu, nasi dagang, keropok lekor (fish sausage).
- **Sarawak:** Sarawak laksa, kolo mee, and layered cake (*kek lapis*).
- **Sabah:** hinava (raw fish cured with lime) and fresh seafood in Kota Kinabalu.
