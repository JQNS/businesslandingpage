## At a glance

| | Flight | ETS train + shuttle | Direct bus | Drive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time from KL | ~75 min in the air | ~6 hrs to Padang Besar, then ~1 hr | 9+ hrs | 6–8 hrs plus border |
| Border | At Hat Yai airport | Padang Besar station | Bukit Kayu Hitam–Sadao | Bukit Kayu Hitam–Sadao |
| Best for | Short trips, most people | Train lovers, no airport hassle | Tight budgets | Families from the north, shopping hauls |
| Watch out for | Fares rise on long weekends | Limited daily departures | Long border queues | Paperwork and holiday queues |

## 1. Fly KLIA → Hat Yai

The quickest option by far. Flights take about 75 minutes, and you clear Thai immigration inside the airport, so you skip the land border queues completely.

**Pro tip:** fares jump on Malaysian long weekends and school holidays. Book early, and compare morning flights so you get a full first day.

## 2. ETS train to Padang Besar, then into Thailand

Take the **KTM ETS** from KL Sentral to **Padang Besar**, about 6 hours. Both Malaysian and Thai immigration are inside Padang Besar station. From there, take the **Thai shuttle train** to Hat Yai Junction (about an hour) or a van to Hatyai town.

- **Good:** comfortable, scenic, no airport security
- **Not so good:** only a few ETS departures a day reach Padang Besar, so check times on the KTMB app before planning

<Callout type="info" title="Direct KL–Hatyai charter trains">
KTMB has run occasional direct charter trains between KL Sentral and Hat Yai on selected dates. Check KTMB's announcements in case more dates are added.
</Callout>

## 3. Direct bus from TBS

Several operators run direct buses from **TBS** to Hatyai, crossing at **Bukit Kayu Hitam–Sadao**. It's the cheapest door-to-door option with no changes, but expect **9 hours or more**, depending on the border.

**Pro tip:** take a daytime bus on a weekday. Friday night and the first day of school holidays bring the longest queues at the border.

## 4. Drive your own car

Driving gives you the most freedom, and room in the boot for your shopping. Northern families from Kedah, Penang and Perlis often do it as a weekend trip. You'll need:

1. **Passport** for every traveller, plus a **TDAC** for each person, including children
2. **Malaysian driving licence** (an International Driving Permit is recommended)
3. **Original vehicle ownership certificate (geran)**. If the car isn't yours, bring the owner's signed authorisation letter and a copy of their IC
4. **Thai compulsory insurance**. Malaysian car insurance doesn't cover Thailand. Shops on the Malaysian side before the border sell it and can fill in your forms
5. **TM2 and TM3 forms** (vehicle and passenger lists)
6. **Temporary import document** from Thai Customs at the border. Keep it safe: you return it when you leave

<Callout type="warn" title="Check the border hours">
Sources disagree on Bukit Kayu Hitam's closing time, and holiday queues can run for hours. Check the current hours before you leave and cross early in the morning.
</Callout>

## Where to stay in Hatyai

Most Malaysians stay in the **city centre**, within walking distance of the markets, food streets and shopping around Lee Gardens. With a car, check that the hotel has parking.
