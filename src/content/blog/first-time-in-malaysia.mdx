## The people: one country, many cultures

Malaysia has about **34.3 million people** (Department of Statistics Malaysia, Q3 2025). Among citizens, the main groups are:

| Group | Share of citizens | You'll notice it in… |
|---|---|---|
| **Malay** | 58.2% | Nasi lemak, songket and batik, kampung houses, Hari Raya |
| **Chinese** | 22.2% | Hokkien, Cantonese and Hakka food, clan houses, Chinese New Year |
| **Other Bumiputera** | 12.3% | The indigenous peoples of Sabah and Sarawak, Gawai and Kaamatan festivals |
| **Indian** | 6.5% | Banana leaf rice, Brickfields, Deepavali, Thaipusam at Batu Caves |

*Source: DOSM Demographic Statistics, Q3 2025.*

A few things that make Malaysia's mix special:

- **Bumiputera** means "sons of the soil". It covers the Malays and the indigenous peoples: the **Orang Asli** of the peninsula and Borneo groups such as the **Kadazan-Dusun, Bajau and Murut** in Sabah and the **Iban, Bidayuh and Melanau** in Sarawak. The Iban are the largest group in Sarawak.
- **Peranakan (Baba-Nyonya)** families in Melaka and Penang descend from Chinese traders who settled here centuries ago. Their food and beaded shoes are a culture of their own.
- **Kristang** Eurasians in Melaka trace their roots to the Portuguese, who arrived in 1511, and still speak their own creole.

Most Malaysians grow up switching between two or three languages, often in a single sentence. That's **Manglish**, and yes, we really do end sentences with *lah*.

## Religion and festivals

Islam is the official religion, and the constitution protects freedom of religion. In the 2020 census, **63.5%** of Malaysians were Muslim, **18.7%** Buddhist, **9.1%** Christian and **6.1%** Hindu. Mosques, Chinese temples, Hindu temples and churches often sit on the same street. In KL, you can see all four within a short walk of Merdeka Square.

Because of that, Malaysia has **a lot** of public holidays. Big ones coming up:

| Festival | Date | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Deepavali** | 8 Nov 2026 | Festival of lights. Brickfields (KL's Little India) glows with kolam art and stalls |
| **Christmas** | 25 Dec 2026 | Huge mall decorations, especially along Bukit Bintang |
| **Thaipusam** | 22 Jan 2027 | Hundreds of thousands of devotees walk to the Batu Caves temple |
| **Chinese New Year** | 6–7 Feb 2027 | Lion dances, lanterns, and KL goes quiet as people return to their hometowns |

Dates follow the official holiday calendar. Thaipusam is a holiday only in some states, including KL and Selangor.

During Hari Raya, Chinese New Year and Deepavali, many families hold an **open house**, where friends of every background (and sometimes strangers) are invited in to eat. If a Malaysian invites you, go. It's the best food you'll have all trip.

## Etiquette: how to be a good guest

Malaysians are relaxed and friendly, and nobody expects visitors to know everything. These habits go a long way:

1. **Take your shoes off** before entering someone's home, and at mosques and many temples. A pile of shoes at the door is your clue.
2. **Dress modestly at religious sites.** Cover your shoulders and knees. Big mosques such as Masjid Negara lend robes and headscarves at the entrance, and Batu Caves rents sarongs at the bottom of the stairs.
3. **Use your right hand** to give, receive and eat. It's polite to pass money or a business card with your right hand, or with both hands.
4. **Point with your thumb,** not your index finger. Many Malays point with the right thumb, fingers folded.
5. **Don't touch anyone's head,** including children's.
6. **Keep public affection low-key.** Holding hands is fine; making out on the LRT is not.
7. **During Ramadan,** avoid eating or drinking in front of people who are fasting where you can. Restaurants stay open, and the evening Ramadan bazaars are brilliant.
8. **No durian on trains or in most hotels.** You'll see the "no durian" signs. Eat it at the stall.

<Callout type="tip" title="Saying hi">
A light handshake is normal, but some Muslims prefer not to shake hands with the opposite sex. Wait to see if a hand is offered. A smile, a nod and a hand over your heart is a warm, safe greeting.
</Callout>

## Before you land: MDAC, visas and money

### Fill in the MDAC arrival card

Since 1 January 2024, most foreign visitors must submit the **Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** online **within 3 days before arrival**. It's **free** and takes about five minutes. Singapore citizens are exempt.

<Callout type="warn" title="Use the official site only">
The only official MDAC site is <strong>imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac</strong>. Lookalike sites charge "service fees" for a form that costs nothing. Check visa-free rules for your passport on the Immigration Department site too.
</Callout>

### Money

- **Ringgit (RM)** is the currency. Cards and phone payments work in malls, chains and most cafés. **DuitNow QR** codes are everywhere.
- **Carry some cash** for hawker stalls, night markets and small kopitiams (coffee shops).
- **Tipping isn't expected.** Sit-down restaurants often add a **10% service charge** plus tax. Hawker prices are what you pay.
- **Hotels charge foreign guests a tourism tax** of RM10 per room per night, collected at check-in or check-out. It's normal, not a scam.

### Plugs and power

Malaysia uses **Type G three-pin plugs** at 240V, like the UK and Singapore. Visitors from Europe, the US, Australia or most of Asia need an adapter.

## Getting around

- **From KLIA:** The **KLIA Ekspres** train reaches KL Sentral in about 30 minutes. The airport bus is the cheapest option.
- **Grab** is the main ride-hailing app for cars and food delivery. Download it before you land.
- **Touch 'n Go card:** The tap card for the LRT, MRT, Monorail, buses and KTM Komuter trains, also used on highway tolls. Buy one at any station customer service counter. Bank cards don't reliably work at train gates yet.
- **Sabah and Sarawak have their own immigration checks.** Even on a domestic flight from KL to Kota Kinabalu or Kuching, foreign visitors have their passport checked again. Keep it in your hand luggage.

## Weather: hot, humid, and an umbrella at 4pm

Malaysia is tropical all year: around **30°C** in the day and humid, with sudden **afternoon thunderstorms**, especially from October to December on the west coast. Storms usually pass within an hour or two, and KL's malls and covered walkways keep you dry.

The **northeast monsoon** (roughly November to March) brings heavy rain to the east coast. Many resorts on islands such as **Perhentian and Redang close**, while **Penang and Langkawi** are in their best season. ## 8 fun facts to impress your Malaysian friends

1. **The Petronas Twin Towers** (452 m) are still the tallest twin towers in the world, joined by a skybridge on levels 41–42.
2. **Merdeka 118** in KL is now the second-tallest building on Earth, at 678.9 m.
3. **Malaysia has a rotating king.** The Yang di-Pertuan Agong is chosen from the nine hereditary Malay state rulers for a five-year term.
4. **There are two Malaysias, geographically:** the peninsula and Malaysian Borneo (Sabah and Sarawak), with the South China Sea in between.
5. **Malaya became independent on 31 August 1957** (Merdeka Day). **Malaysia** was formed on 16 September 1963, when Sabah, Sarawak and Singapore joined. Singapore left in 1965.
6. **Mount Kinabalu** in Sabah (4,095 m) is one of the highest peaks in Southeast Asia, and in 2000 Kinabalu Park and Sarawak's Gunung Mulu became Malaysia's first UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
7. **George Town and Melaka** share one UNESCO World Heritage listing, inscribed in 2008, for their multicultural trading-port heritage.
8. **Visit Malaysia 2026's mascot is the Malayan sun bear,** the world's smallest bear, chosen to highlight wildlife conservation.
