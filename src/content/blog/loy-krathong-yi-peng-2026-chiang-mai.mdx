It's one of the most beautiful nights in Asia, and it's only a short flight from KL. But it's also the night most first-timers get wrong: wrong tickets, wrong area, or hotels booked too late. Here's how to do it properly.

## Loy Krathong vs Yi Peng: what's the difference?

- **Loy Krathong** happens all over Thailand. People float a **krathong**, a small basket of banana leaves, flowers and a candle, on a river to let go of bad luck.
- **Yi Peng** is Chiang Mai's own northern (Lanna) festival, famous for **sky lanterns** (khom loy) and lanterns hung across the old city.

In Chiang Mai they happen together, so you get both.

## The one rule most tourists don't know: where lanterns are allowed

You **can't** release sky lanterns anywhere you like in Chiang Mai. The city restricts it because of air traffic and fire risk, and the airport adjusts flights on festival nights.

The famous mass-release photos come from **ticketed events outside the city**. If releasing a lantern is your dream, book one of these. In town, you can still float a krathong on the Ping River and see the lantern-lit streets for free.

## Lantern event tickets

Ticketed events include transport from Chiang Mai, dinner, cultural shows and lanterns to release. They're expensive, but they're the only legal way to join a mass release, and they sell out.

## What to do for free in town

- **Tha Phae Gate and the old city:** lanterns hung across temples and streets, with parades and stalls.
- **Ping River (around Nawarat Bridge):** the main spot to float your krathong. Buy one from street vendors.
- **Temples at night:** Wat Phan Tao and Wat Chedi Luang look stunning lit with candles. Dress modestly: covered shoulders and knees.

## Getting there from Malaysia

- **Fly KL → Chiang Mai direct.** The easiest option, but book now, because festival-week fares rise fast.
- **Fly via Bangkok.** Often more flight times to choose from. Allow a comfortable gap between flights.
- **Arrive by 23 November** so you're settled before the main nights.

Before you fly, submit your free [TDAC arrival card](/blog/tdac-thailand-digital-arrival-card-malaysians/) and check the [new 30-day visa-free rule](/blog/thailand-entry-rules-malaysians/).

## Where to stay

| Area | Best for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Old City** | First-timers, walking to festivities | Temples, Tha Phae Gate and lantern streets on your doorstep |
| **Riverside (Ping River)** | Floating krathong, views | Closest to the main krathong spot, more upmarket |
| **Nimman** | Cafés, younger crowd | Calmer at night, a short Grab ride to the old city |

Roads around the old city close or jam on festival nights, so staying within walking distance of Tha Phae Gate saves a lot of stress.

## What to pack

- Light layers: November evenings in Chiang Mai are cooler than in KL
- Comfortable walking shoes
- A small torch or power bank (it's dark and crowded away from the main streets)
- Modest clothes for temple visits
