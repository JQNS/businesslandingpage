What the API numbers mean

Malaysia uses the Air Pollutant Index (API). Singapore uses the Pollutant Standards Index (PSI). Both use the same bands:

ReadingStatusWhat to do
0–50🟢 GoodNormal activities
51–100🟡 ModerateSensitive groups should limit long outdoor activity
101–200🟠 UnhealthyReduce outdoor activity and wear an N95 outdoors
201–300🔴 Very unhealthyStay indoors as much as possible and run an air purifier
300+🟣 HazardousAvoid going outside, especially the elderly, children and pregnant women

Check live readings: Malaysia: APIMS (Department of Environment) or the MyIPU app. Singapore: haze.gov.sg or the myENV app.

Who is most at risk?

  • 👶 Children, who breathe faster and take in more polluted air for their size
  • 👵 Elderly people, and anyone with heart or lung conditions
  • 🤰 Pregnant women
  • 🫁 People with asthma, whose attacks can be triggered by haze

10 practical tips to get through the haze

  1. Check the API every morning before planning school runs, exercise or outings.
  2. Keep doors and windows closed and seal gaps with tape or towels if needed.
  3. Run the air-con on recirculate at home and in the car, and replace an old car cabin filter.
  4. Wear an N95 outdoors, with a proper fit and no gaps around the nose.
  5. Move your workouts indoors. Running in haze means breathing in far more particles.
  6. Drink more water to soothe your throat and eyes.
  7. Don’t smoke or burn anything indoors, including candles and incense.
  8. Prepare for home-based learning: devices charged, Wi-Fi working and a quiet study space.
  9. Keep asthma medication within reach and make sure inhalers haven’t expired.
  10. Look out for elderly neighbours and relatives, who may not be checking the news.
When to see a doctor

Seek medical help if you or your child have difficulty breathing, chest pain, wheezing that won’t settle, or a persistent cough or fever. Call 999 (Malaysia) or 995 (Singapore) in an emergency. This guide is general information, not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

When do schools close because of haze in Malaysia?

Under the Education Ministry rule announced on 8 October 2026, schools must close in areas where the API exceeds 180, or stays above 150 for three consecutive days. Affected schools switch to home-based learning.

What API level is unhealthy?

In Malaysia, an API of 101–200 is unhealthy, 201–300 is very unhealthy and above 300 is hazardous. Singapore's PSI uses the same bands.

Do surgical masks protect against haze?

Not well. Haze particles (PM2.5) are tiny. A properly fitted N95 or KN95 mask filters far more of them than a surgical or cloth mask.

Where can I check the API today?

In Malaysia, use the Department of Environment's APIMS website or the MyIPU app. In Singapore, check haze.gov.sg or the NEA app for PSI and PM2.5 readings.
